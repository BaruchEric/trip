# M12 recon — what OSM knows about transit, in four cities

*Run 2026-07-27, before the design, as in M6, M7, M8 and M9. Every number below
comes from a raw response captured to `test/fixtures/m12-transit/`, with
`capture.ts`, `capture-rail.ts` and `capture-retry.ts` beside it carrying the
production queries written out in full, and `analyse.ts` regenerating every
figure from those captures without touching the network.*

## Why this was measured at all

`src/plan/geo.ts` has computed every transit time this project has ever
printed from three constants:

```
transit: haversine × 1.20 ÷ 18 km/h + 6 min per hop
```

M8 measured the walking constants against two routers. It measured **nothing**
about transit, and said so. `--mode=transit` is reachable from `trip set`,
`trip plan` and `trip replan`, so a user can compile an entire itinerary on
those three numbers.

M12's question was whether OSM's own data can do better.

## Finding 0 — the query in the handoff was wrong about the city

The M12 handoff recorded "Overpass, `rel["route"="subway"]` around Chongqing:
**26 relations**". Re-run inside a stated bounding box it returns **28**. Both
numbers are wrong about Chongqing in the same way.

**`route=subway` returns no Line 2 and no Line 3.** Both are straddle-beam
monorails, and OSM tags them `route=monorail`. The captured `ref` list is
1, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 18, the loop, and two suburban lines — lines 2 and 3 are
absent, and so are the stations 李子坝 (Liziba), 牛角沱 and 临江门.

**Liziba is one of the seven places this project has resolved.** Line 2 is the
line that runs through the residential block there — the single most
photographed thing in Chongqing transit. On the subway-only capture, Liziba's
nearest station computed as Eling, 688 m away, because Liziba's own station
was not in the data.

Widening to `route ∈ {subway, monorail, light_rail, tram}`:

| capture | relations | stations | Liziba's nearest station |
|---|---|---|---|
| `route=subway` | 28 | 219 | Eling, 688 m |
| all four rail modes | 38 | 287 | **Liziba, 216 m** |

A model built on the first capture would have routed a traveller around two of
the busiest lines in the city and reported the detour as the answer. **The
first-pass files are kept in the fixture directory, not deleted** — they are
the evidence for this finding, and removing them would leave the recon
asserting the undercount instead of showing it.

This is the M6 rule reaching one level further out than M6 stated it. Evidence
gathered with a query that differs from the production query is evidence about
a different question — and *the recon's own query* is one of the queries that
can be wrong. The mode set is part of the measurement (M9).

## Finding 1 — the relations carry geometry, and carry no time whatsoever

Checked at the TAG level across all 126 route relations in four cities, not
inferred from a count:

| tag | occurrences |
|---|---|
| `interval` | **0** |
| `headway` | **0** |
| `duration` | **0** |
| `opening_hours` | **0** |
| `service_times` | **0** |

A separate Overpass query for `rel[route=…][interval]` inside each city's box
returned **zero elements in all four cities**. What the relations do carry is
`name`, `ref`, `network`, `operator`, `colour`, `from`, `to` — identity, not
schedule.

What IS present and usable is the **ordered stop list**. `out body` emits
relation members in document order, and consecutive stop-role members are the
station sequence:

| city | relations | route masters | stations | directed edges | modes present |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chongqing | 38 | 0 | 287 | 670 | monorail, subway, light_rail |
| Lisbon | 25 | 2 | 177 | 334 | tram, subway, light_rail |
| Bangkok | 18 | 1 | 166 | 308 | subway, monorail |
| Amsterdam | 45 | 0 | 222 | 499 | subway, tram |

So a station graph is buildable — positions, ordering, line membership,
interchanges — and **the time to traverse any edge of it is not in the data.**

Note the route-master column: 0, 2, 1, 0. Route masters are largely unmapped,
so "38 relations" cannot be divided into lines from the data either.

## Finding 2 — the constant is optimistic, and this time it does NOT reverse

Door-to-door station-graph time versus the shipped constant, over the same
seven resolved places per city that M8 and M9 used (42 directed pairs each).
Access and egress walks priced with the production walking model; rail at
30 km/h; 4 minutes per transfer and 4 minutes of initial wait:

| city | n | median signed diff | constant OPTIMISTIC in | graph beats measured walk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chongqing | 42 | **+8.0 min** | 38/42 | 38/42 |
| Lisbon | 42 | **+13.7 min** | 42/42 | 33/42 |
| Bangkok | 42 | **+24.4 min** | 40/42 | 31/42 |
| Amsterdam | 42 | **+11.1 min** | 40/42 | 28/42 |

**This is the opposite of what M9 found for walking.** M8's walking result
reversed sign in three new cities, which is why `calibrate` exists and why the
walking constants were never recalibrated. The transit result does not
reverse: in all four cities the constant under-states door-to-door transit
time, by a median of 8 to 24 minutes, and that is the dangerous direction —
a plan built on it runs late and cascades.

### The two constants OSM cannot supply, swept

Rail speed and transfer penalty are not in the data, so they are not chosen —
they are varied. Cells are "pairs where the constant is optimistic / 42":

| rail km/h | transfer | Chongqing | Lisbon | Bangkok | Amsterdam |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 2 | 38/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 | 39/42 |
| 25 | 4 | 38/42 | 42/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 |
| 25 | 6 | 38/42 | 42/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 |
| 30 | 2 | 33/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 | 39/42 |
| 30 | 4 | 38/42 | 42/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 |
| 30 | 6 | 38/42 | 42/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 |
| 35 | 2 | **24/42** | 39/42 | 38/42 | 38/42 |
| 35 | 4 | 36/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 |
| 35 | 6 | 38/42 | 42/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 |
| 40 | 2 | **22/42** | 37/42 | 38/42 | 38/42 |
| 40 | 4 | 30/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 | 39/42 |
| 40 | 6 | 38/42 | 42/42 | 40/42 | 40/42 |

**The direction survives every cell tested**, but not with equal margin.
Chongqing at 40 km/h with a 2-minute transfer falls to 22/42 — a bare
majority. The finding is "the constant is optimistic", not "the constant is
optimistic by N minutes", and the sweep is why the second claim is not made.

## Finding 3 — the constant's real defect is that it never says "walk"

The constant is a straight line at 18 km/h plus six minutes. Because it has no
idea where any station is, it is faster than walking almost everywhere:

| city | constant says "ride, it is faster" | of those, measured walking really wins | worst understatement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chongqing | 42/42 | 4 | +18 min (Hongya Cave → Kuixinglou) |
| Lisbon | 40/42 | 7 | +30 min (Santa Justa Lift → Alfama) |
| Bangkok | 42/42 | 11 | **+70 min** (Chatuchak Market → Jim Thompson House) |
| Amsterdam | 36/42 | 10 | +33 min (Anne Frank House → Royal Palace) |

The `perHopMinutes: 6` term exists in `geo.ts` with the comment *"this is what
correctly makes transit lose to walking over short distances"*. Measured, it
does not: six minutes is not enough to make the straight-line ride lose, and
transit is recommended over walking in 36 to 42 of every 42 pairs.

The station graph gets this right for a structural reason rather than a tuned
one — when both endpoints' nearest station is **the same station**, the metro
contributes nothing at all and the trip is a walk. That happens in 4/42 pairs
in Chongqing, 6/42 in Bangkok, 2/42 in Amsterdam, 0/42 in Lisbon.

## Finding 4 — Valhalla's `bus` costing, re-probed and still a trap

Re-run 2026-07-27 against `valhalla1.openstreetmap.de`, Hongya Cave → Testbed 2,
reproducing the handoff exactly:

| costing | result |
|---|---|
| `multimodal` | **HTTP 400**, `error_code 170`, "Locations are in unconnected regions" |
| `transit` | **HTTP 400**, same error |
| `bus` | **HTTP 200** — 6.76 km, 13 min |
| `pedestrian` | HTTP 200 — 5.02 km, 77 min |

FOSSGIS Valhalla carries no GTFS feed, so the two transit costings fail
closed, which is the honest failure. **`bus` returning 200 is the trap.** It
is a vehicle routed over bus-legal roads: no stops, no waiting, no transfers,
no timetable. It reports 13 minutes for a trip the pedestrian router puts at
77, and it would look exactly like a transit answer. Anything that quietly
used it would be confidently wrong in the direction of "you have plenty of
time".

It is captured here so the recon can show the trap rather than assert it. Any
production code that calls Valhalla must make `costing: "bus"` unreachable,
and a comment is not a guard.

## What was NOT established

- **Any absolute transit time.** Wait, line-haul speed and transfer cost are
  all absent from OSM. The station graph's minute counts in Finding 2 are
  arithmetic on three assumed constants, and they move by 8+ minutes across
  the swept range. **The graph is evidence about DIRECTION and ORDERING, not
  about duration.** A model's output is not evidence about the model.
- **That the station graph is closer to the truth than the constant.** It is
  better *informed* — it knows where the stations are. Nothing here measured
  either against a real transit trip, because no free router covers these
  cities. This recon compares two models to each other and both to a
  pedestrian measurement; it does not have transit ground truth.
- **Station identity.** The graph groups stops by NAME, which is what makes
  interchanges connect. Two genuinely distinct stations sharing a name would
  be merged into one, and nothing here checks for that.
- **Line frequency, anywhere.** A line running every 3 minutes and one running
  every 30 are identical in this data. For a traveller this is often the
  largest single term, and it is the one thing OSM does not have.
- **Four cities is four cities.** Same caveat M9 recorded: all major,
  well-mapped, tourist-heavy. Nothing here measures a small or poorly mapped
  city, and Chongqing's monorail finding is precisely a case of a well-mapped
  city being mapped in a way the obvious query misses.
- **Whether `route=subway` undercounts elsewhere.** Bangkok's BTS is
  `light_rail` and Amsterdam runs `tram`, so the wide mode set mattered in
  three of four cities. Whether it is ever *too* wide — a tram that no
  traveller would ride between two segments — is untested.
